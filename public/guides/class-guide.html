The idea of the class, in one sentence

Remove one note from a scale and its tritone disappears. Everything else follows from that.

The major scale has one tritone, F–B. Take away F or B and it's gone. No other single note does that. The six notes left are the diatonic hexatonic, and they sound calm, open and "modern". That's why gospel, folk and modern jazz players reach for them. From that one move you get the sound (ear), the reason (theory) and a scale that fits the hands well (piano).

0′–5′EarTheory

1. The hook: remove one note

Everyone hears the difference before anyone names it.

Do
  1. At your piano, play C major up and down, slowly.
  2. Play it again without F.
  3. Ask the room: "Which one felt calmer, less like it wanted to go somewhere?" Hands up for version one, then version two.
Say
  • "A major scale has exactly one unstable interval, F to B. It's called the tritone."
  • "Take away F, and it's gone. Six notes, no tritone. That's a hexatonic scale, and it's today's whole class."

TwistDon't use the word "hexatonic" until minute 5. Let them hear it first, then give it a name.

5′–15′Theory

2. Knock one out

Students predict, then see: only two notes kill the tritone.

Click
  1. /workout → Key C → the Knock one out card → Major.
  2. For each row, press ▶. Before revealing the Tritones column, everyone shows thumbs up (tritone still there) or thumbs down (gone).
  3. Switch to Natural minor and do the same. Then Harmonic minor.
Say
  • "Only the 4 and the 7 work. Every other note leaves the tritone in."
  • "In minor, it's the 2 and the ♭6. Same rule, because minor is just major started from a different note."
  • "Look at 'Also known as': C major without 4 is A minor without ♭6. Same six notes, two names."
  • "Harmonic minor: no row works. It has two tritones that share no note, and one removal can only kill one of them."

15′–25′Ear

3. Ear game: which note is missing?

Hear something that isn't there. It's the hardest listening skill, and the most useful.

Click
  1. /ear. The app plays a major scale with one note left out. Use ▶ Hear it again as often as needed.
  2. On "three", everyone holds up fingers for the missing degree, 2 to 7. Reveal, then Next round →. Play six rounds.
Then
  • Silent singing: everyone sings the full scale on "la" while you play the six-note version on the piano. When your piano skips the note, the room sings it loudly into the gap.
Levels
  • Beginners hum along and just raise a hand at the gap. Advanced students name the letter as well as the degree.

TwistIn silent singing, students supply the missing note with their own voices. That is audiation, hearing a sound in your head, which is more than guessing.

25′–40′PianoTheory

4. At the piano: the minor hexatonic

One scale learned properly: up, down, in thirds, in fourths.

C D E♭ F G A♭ B♭

Click
  1. /live → preset Minor → Space to play and stop. [ and ] change tempo; ↑ ↓ change key.
  2. Preset In fourths, then preset In thirds.
Do
  • Up and down: right hand, then left hand, then both, at 84 bpm.
  • Ring the fourths: left hand holds C, right hand walks the fourths pattern. Every interval rings perfect.
  • Students without keyboards sing it in sargam or sol-fa along with the app.
Say
  • "This is the minor scale without its ♭6. In Carnatic music it's raga Pushpalathika."
  • "Play it in fourths: every single interval comes out perfect, six for six. Try that on the major scale and F–B breaks the chain."
  • "In thirds you get two major thirds, two minor thirds, and two perfect fourths where the gap is."

TwistAsk them to find a scale where every fourth is perfect. Let them try the major scale and fail at F–B, then show the hexatonic.

40′–50′EarTheory

5. The family: blues, gospel, whole tone, augmented

Four more six-note scales, each with a character the ear can grab.

Click
  1. /workout → the named hexatonics buttons: Blues, Gospel / major blues, Whole tone, Augmented. Press ▶ Up on each.
  2. On Blues, point to Same six notes, other names: C Blues = E♭ Major blues.
Say
  • "Blues is minor pentatonic plus ♭5. Gospel is major pentatonic plus ♭3. They're the same six notes, a minor third apart."
  • "Whole tone has no perfect fifth anywhere, and only two of them exist. Augmented is two augmented triads, and four of them exist."
Game
  • Give each scale one word: bite (blues), church (gospel), dream (whole tone), shimmer (augmented). Play one at random and the room calls out the word.

TwistGive each scale its character word before you explain the theory. People remember a word they attached to a sound.

50′–60′PianoTheory

6. Rhythm: groupings that land

Feel why six notes are the right size for rhythm practice.

Click
  1. /live → presets Tisra (3s), Chatusra (4s), Khanda (5s). Watch the resolves in … bars readout before you press Space.
Do
  • The room claps the accent and says it in konnakol: ta-ki-ta, ta-ka-di-mi, ta-di-gi-na-thom.
  • One volunteer plays the scale at the piano while the room claps.
Say
  • "Groups of 3, 4 or 6 in sixteenths: this scale lands back on beat 1 in 3 bars. The major scale takes 7 to 21 bars."
  • "Groups of 5 in triplets land in 5 bars. Six shares factors with almost everything; seven shares them with nothing."

TwistThis is the argument for teaching rhythmic grouping on a six-note scale instead of the major scale. A whole room can land a 3- or 5-bar cycle together; they can't land a 21-bar one.

60′–72′TheoryPianoEar

7. Harmonise it, then the Barry Harris lens

The chords live inside the scale, and two notes have no chord of their own.

Click
  1. /workout → Key C → Major, no 4 (C D E G A B).
  2. Harmonisation card: tap the chips to hear each chord with the melody note on top. Point out that D and B say "rooted: none".
  3. Barry Harris lens → the C6 card → ▶ Harmonise the scale.
Say
  • "Four triads live inside: C, Am, Em, G. And C6 is the same chord as Am7: one chord, two names."
  • "D and B are the only notes with no triad of their own."
  • "Barry Harris alternates a C6 chord with B diminished 7th. D and B are exactly the diminished notes. That's why they sound like passing notes: they are."
Do
  • Right hand plays C–D–E as a melody. Left hand alternates C6 (on C and E) and B°7 (on D).

TwistTell them this is week one of a Barry Harris thread. Show /harmony?tab=barry for ten seconds as a teaser for next time.

8. Create: roll the dice

Everyone makes something of their own before they leave.

Click
  1. /workout → Roll a challenge → 🎲 Roll. It picks a key, a scale, a drill and one creative rule.
  2. Optional backing: /improvise → Vamp Drone or I – vi, about 72 bpm.
Do

  • Two volunteers perform for the room.

TwistThe creative rule is the help. "Land on a chord tone on every beat 1" makes a beginner sound deliberate.

82′–90′Theory

9. Recap, homework, questions

Five sentences to remember and a week of 20-minute practice.

Do
  • Read the five takeaways below out loud. Share the app link. Hand out the homework.
  • Take questions.
  • End on sound: play the minor hexatonic in fourths one last time.

Five things to remember

  1. Remove the 4 or the 7 from major (the 2 or the ♭6 from minor) and the tritone is gone. No other single note does it.
  2. Major without 4 and its relative minor without ♭6 are the same six notes.
  3. Play the diatonic hexatonic in fourths and every interval is perfect.
  4. Blues and gospel are one set of notes, a minor third apart.
  5. Harmonise the Barry Harris way: chord tones get the 6th chord, the other notes get the diminished.

Homework · 20 minutes a day

Day 1Minor hexatonic up and down in C, G and D.
Day 2Knock one out in G major. Sing the missing note before each row.
Day 3In fourths, all twelve keys.
Day 4Blues, then gospel, over the 12-bar blues.
Day 5Groups of 5 in triplets, counting ta-di-gi-na-thom.
Day 6Harmonise C D E G A B with C6 and B°7.
Day 7Roll the dice three times. Record the last one.

Say it right

Six lines people get wrong about this material

SayDon't say
"Removing it takes away the tritone.""Nothing dissonant is left."
"The gospel scale is 1 2 ♭3 3 5 6, the major blues."Calling the no-4 scale "the gospel scale".
"The Sunday Scale is major without its 7: 1 2 3 4 5 6." (Peter Martin, Open Studio)Using "Sunday Scale" for the other rotations.
"The minor hexatonic is raga Pushpalathika.""It's Sriranjani." (Sriranjani drops Pa.)
"This is our connection to Barry Harris's system.""Barry Harris taught a six-note scale." He didn't.
"For centuries singers learned on six notes, Guido's ut re mi fa sol la.""Music had six notes before seven."

If something goes wrong

  • No sound: click any ▶ once (browsers need one tap first), check the laptop isn't muted, then reload the tab.
  • Sound stopped by itself: switching away from a tab stops playback on purpose. Press play again.
  • Internet drops: every segment works at your own piano. The app is the backup, not the lesson.
  • Clock wrong: use ← → on /class to jump to the segment you're actually on.

Running long or short

  • Late by 10: in segment 5 skip Augmented, and in segment 8 do pairs without performances.
  • Late by 5: play four ear-game rounds instead of six.
  • Early: /workout → One note away. Press ▶ on a neighbour; the room names the note that changed.
