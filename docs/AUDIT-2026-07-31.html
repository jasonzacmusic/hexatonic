01What is already excellent — verified, not assumed

I read every theory file line by line and re-derived the maths by hand. This engine is the real thing — most scale apps get half of this wrong.

Note spelling engine

Notes are (letter, accidental, octave) — never bare MIDI numbers. Every key spells correctly: the parent scale is built first, then the note is removed. No E♯-where-F-belongs anywhere.

Barry Harris module — exact

All four sixth-diminished scales correct, including the trap everyone falls into (the dominant scale uses A♭, not A) and the 7♭5 scale's six-transposition tritone symmetry. Checked note by note.

Ragas & talas — verified

All eleven ragas checked against the swara table (Pushpalathika, Sriranjani, Marwa, vakra ragas…), sapta talas computed from real anga structure, konnakol syllables match our locked reference.

The resolution solver

The LCM mathematics of groupings-vs-bar is right, including full-resolve vs accent-resolve. This remains the app's moat — nothing on the market does it.

Set theory

Prime forms, interval vectors, Forte names — I re-derived 6-Z47 (blues) and the augmented-pair parity rule by hand. Correct, including the subtle normal-order edge cases.

Audio architecture

Lookahead scheduler on the audio clock, playback-lifecycle guarantee with a contract test, resilient sample loading with pitched fallback. Professional-grade for a web app.

02Nine bugs found — all fixed and live

Shipped to main this morning; Vercel deployed automatically.

FIXED

The click ignored your meter

In 5/4, 7/4, 6/8 or any tala, the strong click still landed every 4 beats — the engine was never told the bar length. Downbeats now land where the meter says.

FIXED

The staff always engraved 4/4

Pick 7/8 or Khanda Ata and the notation still drew 4/4 barlines with a wrong time signature. The score now engraves the real meter — correct signature, correct barring, correct beaming.

FIXED

MIDI grading existed but was never on the page

A complete, tested play-along grading system (notes, accents, timing, early/late bias) was built and then never mounted anywhere. It now lives on /practice — plug in a MIDI keyboard, play the drill, get graded.

FIXED

Wrong notes were reported with the wrong name

The grade report named the note you were supposed to play as the note you played. It now names what you actually played — and shows plain note names, never raw MIDI numbers.

FIXED

Raga playback never reached the upper Sa

The aroha–avaroha preview jumped straight from Ni back down without landing the top Sa — musically wrong in any tradition. It now plays the true line: S R G M P N Ṡ, then down.

FIXED

Barry Harris "run the movement" wouldn't stop

Navigating away mid-run left chords sounding for seconds. The run now cancels on navigation, on scale change, and on re-click.

FIXED

Changing meter mid-play desynced the display

Switching meter or editing a custom scale while playing kept the old audio running against new visuals. Playback now stops cleanly on any musical change.

FIXED

Home page hydration error

The chromatic ring rendered slightly different floating-point coordinates on server and browser — a console error on every load. Coordinates are now rounded and deterministic.

SHIPPED

The major blues scale — see below

Not a bug — a missing scale. Now live.

03The blues answer

You hadn't missed it — you had exactly half of it.

The minor blues hexatonic (1 ♭3 4 ♭5 5 ♭7) was already a family. What was missing was the major blues — 1 2 ♭3 3 5 6, the gospel scale — the sound of gospel piano, Ray Charles, Oscar Peterson. It was even name-checked in the code's own comments and never made selectable.

Shipped today: Major blues hexatonic is now a first-class family on /scales, /practice and /improvise, with the teaching point built in: C major blues and A minor blues are the same six notes (set class 6-Z47, a minor third apart) — one scale, two homes, which is the entire secret of blues piano vocabulary.

What's still missing for the blues is not a scale — it's the form: a 12-bar backing lane, shuffle feel, and dominant-7 harmony that deliberately steps outside the scale. That's item one of the pipeline below.

04The pipeline — by the four ways you practise

Organised by your own frame: intervallically, chordally, scalarly, plus rhythm — and then the two missing senses: ear and eye. Ranked within each lane.

A · The blues, properly

BIGGEST WIN

12-bar blues lane in Improvise

A real I7–IV7–V7 twelve-bar with shuffle feel, walking bass and a "quick change" toggle. The vamp engine currently only plays chords inside the scale — the blues teaches the opposite lesson: one scale over moving dominant harmony. This needs a small second vamp lane that knows chords the scale doesn't contain, and it unlocks the single most-requested practice context in existence.

Also: swing/shuffle subdivision feel for the drill itself (straight vs swung 8ths), and call-and-response mode — the app plays two bars, leaves two bars of silence over the band.

B · Intervallic practice

ADD

Mixed-interval and enclosure patterns

The skip cycles (3rds/4ths/5ths/6ths) are excellent. Missing: compound patterns — "up a 4th, down a step", enclosures (approach the target from above and below), and the pivot exercise (one note held, the other voice walking). These are the bebop & Carnatic datu bridge.

C · Chordal practice

ADD

Arpeggio ladder through every available chord

The app already finds every chord in the scale — but there's no drill that plays them in sequence as arpeggios (C → Am → G → Em, up and down, resolved against the bar like everything else). One new pattern type, huge payoff.

ADD

Inversions & voice-leading trainer

Drill each triad through its inversions; then the smart version — connect chord to chord with the smallest possible motion. The Barry Harris "borrowing" engine is already built and tested in the code but has no UI — expose it as the four-voice movement drill it is.

D · Scalar practice

ADD

True Janta (doubled notes) & Datu (zigzag) patterns

The varisai ladder currently approximates janta with cells — real janta is CC DD EE GG AA BB, and real datu varisai jumps S–G, R–M, G–P. Both are one-line pattern generators in the existing engine, and they make the Carnatic ladder authentic instead of approximate.

ADD

Rotation gauntlet

Same tonic, all six rotations of the hexachord back to back, four bars each — the fastest way to actually hear that six scales are one object. The engine can already do it; it just needs a preset chain.

E · Rhythmic workouts

ADD

Tihai generator

The signature Carnatic ending — one phrase played three times, landing exactly on sam. It is the same LCM mathematics the resolution solver already does, run backwards: give me a phrase length and a tala, and it computes the gap so the third repetition lands on the one. Nobody else has this. It is the resolution solver's natural sequel and a YouTube lesson goldmine.

ADD

Gati bhedam & polyrhythm click

Switch gati mid-stream (4s → 3s → 5s over a constant beat) as a scheduled sequence, and a two-layer click (3:2, 4:3) under any drill. Both fit the existing scheduler.

F · Ear training — the biggest empty room

NEW WING

"Which note is missing?"

The perfect Hexatonic ear game, straight from the app's own thesis: it plays a hexachord, you name the degree that was removed. Nobody else can even pose this question — it falls directly out of the omission engine. Start here.

ADD

Interval, chord-quality and mode quizzes

Play two notes → name the interval; play a chord from the scale's own harmony → name it (including the C6 = Am7 flip); play a rotation → name the mode. The MIDI grading that went live today is the answer-input system for all of these — answer by playing, not by clicking.

G · Visualization

ADD

Guitar fretboard view

The ring, keyboard and engraved staff are strong. The missing instrument is guitar — a fretboard with the same removed-note language would double the audience of every page. One component, reused everywhere.

ADD

Circle-of-fifths overlay & falling-note mode

The ring already speaks chromatically; a fifths-mode toggle shows why the hexachord is a stack of five fifths. And a falling-note lane (like our MIDI visualizer) during playback turns Presenter mode into B-roll-ready footage for lessons.
