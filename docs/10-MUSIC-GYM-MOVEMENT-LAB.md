## The classroom idea worth building around

The lesson found one physical practice idea at two scale sizes:

1. **Six notes become two triads.** Take alternate degrees of the ordered
   hexatonic. The two triads interlock through root position, first inversion and
   second inversion while every voice rises by one scale step.
2. **Eight notes become two four-note shapes.** The genuine symmetric octatonic
   splits into two diminished-seventh sets, each moving through four inversions.
3. **Block and arpeggio are two views of the same movement.** One hand can block
   while the other arpeggiates; the exercise should eventually grade both.
4. **Chord speed is melody speed.** The goal is not slow accompaniment changes;
   it is fluent, accented chord movement on every pulse.

The B-flat classroom example is:

`Bb C D Eb F G` → `Bb · Cm · Bb/D · Cm/Eb · Bb/F · Cm/G`

That scale is the **Ionian/Mixolydian hexatonic** (`1 2 3 4 5 6`, major
hexatonic no 7). The same movement is now computed, not hard-coded, in all
twelve keys and all six rotations.

## The three systems must stay separate

The class contained strong practical material, but three names were blended.
The app and the future lesson should correct them cleanly rather than repeat the
confusion.

| System | What it is | Interlocked shapes |
|---|---|---|
| `1 2 3 4 5 6` | Ionian/Mixolydian hexatonic; major hexatonic no 7 | two triads |
| Whole-half / half-whole | the symmetric octatonic; three transpositions | two diminished-seventh sets |
| Barry Harris sixth-diminished | four eight-note, non-symmetric families | a 6th or dominant set + related diminished 7th |

Permanent corrections:

- Do **not** call `1 2 3 4 5 6` the gospel scale. The gospel scale is the major
  blues collection `1 2 b3 3 5 6`.
- Do **not** call it the Sunday scale. That is Peter Martin's separate term for a
  different six-note collection whose exact content has not been verified here.
- Barry Harris has **four** sixth-diminished families, not two: major 6, minor 6,
  dominant 7 and dominant 7 flat 5.
- Barry's collections contain eight notes but are **not** the symmetric
  octatonic. The movement, not the note count, is the teaching point.
- `C6 = Am7` and `Cm6 = Am7b5` are pitch-class-set equivalences. The latter is
  not the “relative minor” of C minor; it is an equivalent four-note reading.

## Feature bank for the next pass

Ordered by value for the proposed YouTube lesson and NSM workshop:

1. **Presenter preset for the movement lab.** Two enormous chord shapes, active
   voices, inversion name and a no-dialog keyboard shortcut layout for filming.
2. **MIDI chord grading.** Grade the complete voicing, bass note, inversion,
   timing and accent—not merely whether the right pitch classes appeared.
3. **Two-hand mode.** Left hand block + right hand scale; left hand block + right
   hand arpeggio; swap hands; contrary motion.
4. **Accent engine for chord movement.** Run the inversion ladder in 3s, 4s and
   5s so the chord accent phases against the bar exactly as the scalar engine does.
5. **Paired-key practice.** Offer the class strategies explicitly: equal
   sharp/flat counts (D and B-flat), same-letter neighbours (D and D-flat), and
   keyboard-shape opposites.
6. **Assignment sheet.** One teacher link containing the scale, chord pair,
   voicing style, direction, tempo and target repetitions.
7. **Fingering layer.** Teacher-authored fingering rather than a guessed universal
   answer, because the transcript itself shows that the two-octave fingering was
   not yet settled.

## YouTube / public-class seed — not yet a final script

The strongest promise is not “learn another scale.” It is:

> Learn two chord shapes so deeply that they begin to sing like a melody.

A clean workshop arc is:

1. Six notes, two triads, three inversions.
2. Change the key without changing the method.
3. Add accents and melody-speed movement.
4. Expand to eight notes and two diminished-seventh sets.
5. Separate symmetric octatonic from Barry Harris, then demonstrate one Barry
   sixth-diminished movement correctly.
6. End with guided improvisation over the movement rather than another scale run.
