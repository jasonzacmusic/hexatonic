## New lesson territory

The new organizing idea is **exact cover**:

> Two chord shapes share zero pitch classes, and their union accounts for every note in the six- or eight-note collection.

The app proves both conditions before presenting a pair.

### Best new headline

**Major without 3: IV + V.** In C, F major plus G major produces `C D F G A B`. It is immediately playable, uses two familiar shapes, and creates a suspended major world because the tonic's third is the omitted note. This adjacent-triad method belongs to documented jazz triad-pair practice, but this specific musical/theory framing did not appear in Jason's 2025 lesson.

### Strong supporting examples

1. **Whole tone = two augmented triads a whole step apart.**
2. **Augmented scale = two augmented triads a minor third apart.**
3. **Chromatic hexatonic = two major triads a semitone apart.**
4. **Petrushka collection = two major triads a tritone apart.**
5. **True symmetric diminished = two diminished-seventh chords.**
6. **Barry Seventh Diminished and Seventh Flat Five Diminished.** These complete the four-family Barry application; the 2025 lesson covered only major 6 and minor 6.

## The exhaustive diatonic result

In any seven-note diatonic scale, adjacent diatonic triads are disjoint. Each pair therefore produces a six-note collection that omits exactly one scale degree:

| pair | omitted degree | lesson value |
|---|---:|---|
| I + ii | 7 | already taught as Sunday Scale |
| I + vii° | 6 | new theory discovery |
| ii + iii | 1 | new rootless colour |
| iii + IV | 2 | new theory discovery |
| IV + V | 3 | strongest new headline |
| V + vi | 4 | same pitch set Jason already taught as Am + G |
| vi + vii° | 5 | new theory discovery |

All seven belong in the app. They should not all be marketed as equally popular: the Pair Atlas distinguishes documented practice, specialist repertoire, and theory discoveries.

## Recommended two-part YouTube structure

### Part 1 — The Chord-Pair Atlas

Hook: "I taught two chords through every inversion. But I missed the rule that finds every scale where it works."

1. State and demonstrate the exact-cover test.
2. Build F + G and discover C major without E.
3. Practise the scale one octave, two octaves, each chord's inversions, and the alternating ladder.
4. Reveal all seven diatonic omissions, but play only the most musical examples.
5. End with the augmented/whole-tone parity surprise.

### Part 2 — Eight notes, two four-note shapes

1. Keep symmetric diminished separate from Barry Harris.
2. Practise true octatonic as two diminished sevenths through four inversions.
3. Recap the previously taught Barry major/minor 6 families quickly.
4. Teach the genuinely new Seventh Diminished and Seventh Flat Five Diminished families.
5. Add borrowing and the related-dominant family as the practical jazz payoff.

## Research lineage

- Gary Campbell, *Triad Pairs for Jazz* (Alfred): deriving pairs from chord-scales, practice patterns, and application.
- Open Studio, *Triad Pair Training Workbook*: chord, up/down, and triplet practice across keys.
- University of Iowa, *Symmetrical Scale*: augmented hexatonic as two augmented triads.
- Integrated Music Theory, *Non-diatonic Scales*: every octatonic collection divides into two fully diminished seventh chords.
- Barry Harris Institute and Howard Rees/Alan Kingstone lineage for the four sixth-diminished families.
