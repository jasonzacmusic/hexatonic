# Hexatonic foundation repairs — 25 July 2026

The audit is preserved separately in `docs/CODEX_AUDIT_FINDINGS.md`. It describes
the production app before any repair. This note records what changed and what
still remains.

## What students will notice

- Play now produces the sampled piano at the written octave, not silence an
  octave above the score.
- Stop stops both piano and click sounds, including sounds that had already been
  scheduled a few seconds ahead.
- Share links with bad or incomplete values recover to safe defaults.
- Remote keys use real key signatures and normal accidental memory.
- Short scores no longer sit in a huge empty frame; 35-bar scores display all
  35 bars instead of silently cutting off after bar 24.
- The staff now belongs visually to the dark app rather than appearing on a
  separate cream sheet.
- Phone practice has three focused control tabs and a persistent Play/tempo bar.
- Presenter labels are larger, its transport no longer changes width while the
  piano loads, and the final landing has a stronger reduced-motion-safe cue.
- The playable piano and generated score now make sense to keyboard and screen
  reader users.

## What changed under the surface

- First-load audio calls share one loading promise and failed loads can retry.
- Every scheduled piano source and click oscillator is tracked and cancelled.
- Playback, highlighting and notation use the same untransposed note data.
- Tempo, loop, click or drill changes stop the old scheduler before new settings
  can disagree with sound already in flight.
- Service-worker caches carry the build commit, retire only old Hexatonic
  caches, and no longer leave returning students on a permanent `v1`.
- Archivo, Cormorant and IBM Plex Mono are bundled with the build instead of
  depending on Google Fonts at runtime.
- CI now type-checks, runs all tests and builds every pull request and every push
  to `main`.
- Vitest, PostCSS and Sharp were patched. `npm audit` reports zero known
  vulnerabilities.

## Deliberately not built

The prompt explicitly allows a roadmap feature to be replaced by a more urgent
audit finding. That exception was used because MIDI grading cannot be trusted
until the app's piano, timing and stop behavior are trustworthy.

MIDI grading, improvisation mode, non-4/4 UI, triad-pair UI and PDF export remain
roadmap work. They were not squeezed into this repair branch.

Three audit items also remain:

1. Mobile LCP is still 4.7 seconds under Lighthouse throttling.
2. Long drills still materialise a repeated note array. Current sizes are safe,
   but a cyclic representation would be cleaner.
3. GitHub `main` is not protected. CI is now present, but repository branch
   protection is an administrator setting rather than an application-code fix.

## Follow-up playback hardening

The first repair fixed the confirmed silent-master and leaked-click defects, but
one professional-standard failure remained: playback still waited for all 17
piano files, and one failed response rejected the entire transport.

The follow-up repair makes audio network-independent:

- Play starts its count-in immediately while required piano samples load first
  and the rest continue in the background.
- Each sample succeeds or fails independently. One missing file cannot mute the
  other sixteen.
- Until a nearby piano sample is ready, the engine uses a short, exact-pitch
  triangle fallback at the correct MIDI frequency rather than going silent.
- Missing samples are retried on later Play or preview gestures and replace the
  fallback automatically once decoded.
- Browser-blocked AudioContext state produces an honest instruction instead of
  showing a false playing state.
- Note, scale and chord preview controls use one rejection-safe path, so a
  browser or network error cannot leak an unhandled promise or leave a button
  permanently busy.

The suite now passes 71 tests. New coverage proves complete network loss,
single-file loss, progressive retry, fallback pitch, fallback cancellation and
blocked-browser handling.

Browser fault injection independently confirmed the same behavior in the
production build:

- With only `C2.mp3` blocked, Play remained active, the healthy piano samples
  sounded through the 0.85 master bus, and no audio alert or console error
  appeared.
- With all 17 piano requests blocked, the count-in and notes still ran. The
  fallback produced the exact C4, D4, E4, G4, A4 and B4 frequencies.
- After restoring the network without reloading the app, the next Play retried
  the missing files and automatically returned to piano sample playback.
